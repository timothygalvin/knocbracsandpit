## The four supports he already has — this is the biggest design input

He will be running these alongside the course, and they change what the
course is *for*:

1. **Duolingo** — spaced drilling of vocabulary and forms.
2. **Videos** — listening input.
3. **Reading** — children's books and simple poetry.
4. **A Russian-speaking community** — live speaking practice.

Design the course so it does **not** compete with any of them. Between
them they cover drill, listening, authentic text and speech. What none of
them gives an adult returner is *explanation, structure and progression* —
so the platform's job is the **spine**: it explains the systems his
break-map shows are broken, sequences the work, and sends him out to the
other four with a specific weekly assignment. Concretely:

- **Do not build vocabulary drills.** Duolingo wins and he will do it
  anyway. Own what Duolingo is bad at: *why* — case logic, aspect, motion.
  That is also exactly where his break-map points.
- **Do not design around audio.** The platform is static and has no asset
  hosting (`docs/TODO.md`). Videos are his listening channel; the course
  should frame and schedule them ("this week, watch X and listen for the
  instrumental"), not try to deliver them.
- **Reading is the best fit you have.** `read` and `reveal` blocks can work
  on real excerpts, and simple poetry is short, memorable, repays rereading
  and carries culture for free. Strongly consider building sessions around
  authentic text rather than invented sentences.
- **The community is the summit.** The house `summit` pattern already ends
  every session with something to do with a human — for the kids it is the
  dinner table. For him it becomes "say this, this week, to a real person,
  and report what happened". That is the most valuable block in the course
  and it costs nothing to build.

Recommend in the plan which of the four each unit leans on, so he knows
what the course expects of him outside it.

## A platform gap you must design around

Language learning needs **spaced review** and the platform has no
scheduler — progress is done-flags and nothing resurfaces on a timer. The
nearest existing mechanism is the house `rematch` pattern (a miss returns
later as a boss question). For this course that needs generalising in
*content*, not code: e.g. every unit's first session reactivates the
previous unit's items. Say how you intend to handle it; do not assume an
engine change, and if you think one is genuinely required, escalate it
rather than designing against vapour.
