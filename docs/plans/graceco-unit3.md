So this unit, non-negotiably:
1. Every scenario in **prose first**, then a table. Never a bare data line.
2. Every year named in full in the label — "after year 3", never "after 3".
3. Big round numbers (£100, £200, £500) and her existing gears (10%, 5%,
   20%, 25%, 50%).
4. Short sentences, one idea per line. That is about sentence *construction*
   — the teaching itself must be as thorough as Jim's. Her sessions ran at a
   third of his teaching words per question and that was the Unit 2 defect.

## Arc

| id | title | time | one new idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| w3s1 | Rent on Money | about 35 min | interest is rent on money — a % of the amount, each year |
| w3s2 | The Snowball | about 35 min | year two charges interest on year one's interest |
| w3s3 | Market Story: The 0% Offer | about 30 min | nothing new — the analyst reads the small print |

### w3s1 · Rent on Money
- Interest as **rent**: you pay rent to use someone's house, and you pay
  interest to use someone's money. It runs both ways — the bank pays *you*
  to borrow yours.
- Straight off her percentage gears: 10% of £200 is £20, so £200 saved at
  10% earns £20 a year. No new arithmetic, only a new name for it.
- Reveal (the surprise): the same £20 is a *cost* if you are the borrower.
  One number, two opposite feelings, depending which side of the desk you
  sit. Analyst framing empowers her here.
- Boss: a year of saving and a year of borrowing side by side, as a table.
- ★: work backwards — the interest is known, find the rate. (Her margin
  move from w2s2, in a new costume.)
- Summit: why does a bank pay you 2% and charge someone else 6%? What is
  the 4% for? (That gap is the whole business of banking, and she already
  understands margin.)

### w3s2 · The Snowball
- One new idea: **compound** interest. £100 at 10% earns £10 in year one.
  In year two the 10% is charged on £110, not £100 — so £11, not £10.
- Reveal: the pattern over three years, forced from a table she fills in
  before the word "compound" is used.
- Keep the numbers kind: £100 at 10% gives 110, 121, 133.10. Two years for
  the spine, the third year as the stretch.
- Then the mirror, and this is where her contagion knowledge pays: a **debt**
  snowballs the same way, only against you. Same maths, opposite sign —
  Unit 1's negatives earning their keep again.
- Boss: compare £500 saved for 2 years at 10% against the same £500
  borrowed for 2 years at 10%. Identical arithmetic, opposite outcome.
- ★: the sting — which is bigger, 20% for one year or 10% for two? (121
  versus 120 on £100: compounding does NOT beat a bigger rate here, which is
  a better lesson than the tidy answer.)
- Summit: the "rule of 72" as dinner-table magic — divide 72 by the rate to
  get the doubling time. Does it work on her numbers?

### w3s3 · Market Story: The 0% Offer
Story session, no new machinery. Ends the unit.
- A real credit-card offer: 0% for 12 months. Why is it free? It is not.
- The three things the analyst checks: what happens in month 13, what the
  minimum payment actually pays off, and the fee for taking the offer.
- Spine: her percentage gears plus w3s2's snowball.
- Her verdict as analyst, in writing, on whether to take the offer.
- `UNIT 3 COMPLETE 🍋✓`, teasing Unit 4 · The Trading Floor.

## Deliberately NOT in this unit

- APR as a formula. The *idea* that the advertised rate hides compounding is
  in scope; the calculation is not.
- Mortgages and amortisation — Unit 4 or later.
- Anything needing a calculator. If the arithmetic is not doable in her
  head with her gears, the numbers are wrong.
