## Findings

1. **The trips left more than expected.** A0 perfect (14/14),
   and the A1 she attempted was ALL correct — including helado, agua,
   leche (not cognates) and producing «tengo diez años» by copying the
   pattern on the page. The pattern-copy skill the course relies on is
   already working.
2. **She knew «la cuenta»** ("the cheque") — an A2 money word, our
   accept list just lacked the British spelling. The analyst identity
   already reaches into Spanish.
3. **The numbers wall, then the cascade.** diez → "8???" (one real
   miss), and from that moment the whole strand went "idk" — and
   rated 1/5 for fun. By the postcard she was idk-ing words she had
   already answered correctly earlier (helado). This is a confidence
   cascade, not ten gaps. Jim's w2s2 taught the same lesson.
4. **She never used the Skip button.** Eleven typed "idk"s, zero
   skips. The affordance failed; "idk" was her skip. Fix at content
   level (see accommodations), and read idk = skip in all her future
   results.
5. **She named her own course** (the percentages pattern again):
   favourite question = Carmen's «Me llamo» "even though I couldn't
   understand it… it would really set me up for real life"; wants to
   "talk to locals, talk to kids, and understand grammar better".
   Conversation with kids is the course; grammar gets visible wins.

## Architecture

10 weeks · 5 units · 3 × ~35 min/week · session types per Playbook:
(a) new idea, (b) practice in the travel world, (c) story/summit.
Checkpoints (cp1, cp2 — `kind:"checkpoint"`) after Units 2 and 4.
Every session: early win first, confidence-cascade guard (any
likely-miss question is followed by a guaranteed win, never by a
harder one), skip framed as smart packing.

## Units

- **Unit 1 · Poolside** (talk to kids — her own goal). Me llamo /
  ¿cómo te llamas? / tengo X años / sí/no/hola stock, THE COGNATE
  REVEAL ("you already know hundreds of Spanish words") as the first
  session's surprise. Carmen returns from the postcard as the pool
  friend. Rematch: nueve; gender jacket (el/la) as a reveal, not a
  rule. ⟨oral⟩ speaking dares sized by willingness score.
- **Unit 2 · The Café** (ordering = her café-strand joy, 4/5).
  quiero / por favor / la cuenta ★ (her word — open with it as a
  WIN, not a lesson). **Numbers 1–20 re-enter here disguised as
  prices in euros** — money framing, big round numbers, her 10%
  gears… in Spanish. Rematches: diez (the "8???" question, labelled
  as the rematch it is), cuatro euros, quince, zumo de naranja.
- **cp1 · Checkpoint: The First Trip** — Units 1–2 + the A2 seeds
  (cien, dónde está).
- **Unit 3 · Postcards** (reading, rebuilt). One postcard per
  session, 100% planted words, detective framing, explicit
  look-back training ("the answer is always IN the postcard —
  go steal it"). Rematches: Málaga, hace sol, helado questions.
- **Unit 4 · ¿Dónde está?** (getting around + the grammar she asked
  for). dónde/qué/cuántos questions; es/está and tengo/tienes as
  named tools ("grammar gears"), always introduced by pattern first.
  Rematch: ¿dónde está la playa?
- **cp2 · Checkpoint: Ready to Fly** — cumulative; mañana, hermano
  seeds return.
- **Unit 5 · Grace en España** (capstone). Scenario sessions chaining
  it all: arrive, order, chat, pay, say goodbye. Final summit: run a
  whole café scene out loud with Dad as the waiter.
