## The lesson w2s2 teaches, applied here

w2s2 introduced a SECOND big idea (running the machine backwards) at the
midpoint, then stacked rule-writing, a boss and a ★ on top. That is where
he stopped. So, for every session in this unit:

1. **One new idea per session**, introduced in the first third.
2. The rest of the session is that same idea working harder — never a
   second conceptual turn after the halfway point.
3. Any inverse/backwards move gets its **own session**, not a late reveal.
4. Difficulty rises through *context*, not through new machinery.

## Arc

### w3s1 · The Mixing Room
World: the expedition's mixing room — chalk and resin for grip, fuel and
water for the stove, gas mixes for altitude. Chemistry hook per his style
sheet (mixtures), no coding framings.

- Ratio as **parts**: 2 : 3 means two scoops to three, at any size.
- The reveal (the surprise): **2 : 3 and 4 : 6 are the same mix.** Doubling
  both sides changes the amount, not the mixture. Forced by a pattern of
  mixes that all taste/grip the same before the rule is stated.
- Simplifying a ratio = dividing both parts by the same number (callback:
  Unit 1's division, and the common-factor instinct).
- Sharing a total in a ratio: total parts first, then one part, then each
  share. This is deliberately the *same move* w3s2 generalises.
- Boss: a three-part mix (e.g. 2 : 3 : 5) shared out of a real total.
- ★: a mix given as a ratio where the TOTAL is unknown but one part is
  known — turning the handle the other way. Flagged ★ and skippable; the
  full inverse treatment waits for w3s2.
- Summit: two mixes that look different but are the same (6 : 9 vs 4 : 6) —
  and one that looks close but isn't (6 : 9 vs 4 : 7). How would you
  convince Dad, without a calculator?

### w3s2 · Scaling the Recipe
- One new idea: **find one part, then multiply.** The unitary method,
  named, with a function-machine feel he already owns from w2s2 — but
  forwards only, deliberately.
- Scale a recipe/mix up and down (whole-number and then awkward factors).
- Direct proportion in words: twice the climbers, twice the fuel.
- Boss: best value — two gas canisters at different sizes and prices,
  decided by cost per unit. Commercially real, and the move Grace's course
  makes in money terms (nice cross-course symmetry, worth a one-line nod).
- ★: a scaling that does NOT work (time for four climbers to pitch a tent)
  — the sting. Not everything is proportional. Prepares Unit 4 modelling.
- Summit: the £ per gram question in the kitchen cupboard, as fieldwork.

### w3s3 · Summit Puzzle: The Oxygen Mix
One rich problem, session-long, no new machinery.
- Climbers above 7,000 m breathe a blended mix; the expedition has
  cylinders of two different blends and must hit a target blend.
- Spine: ratio → total parts → one part → scale (every move from w3s1/w3s2).
- Elegant payoff: the blend that can be hit *exactly* with whole cylinders,
  and the proof that one target blend is impossible — a divisibility sting
  of the same family as w2s1's "does it ever hit zero exactly?", which he
  got right.
- Must carry a ★★ and a summit worthy of the dinner table.
- Ends the unit: done-screen reads `UNIT 3 COMPLETE 🏔️✓` and teases
  Unit 4 · The Modelling Workshop.

## Deliberately NOT in this unit

- Percentages as a topic (that is Grace's spine; Jim meets them in Unit 4).
- Inverse proportion. It belongs with modelling in Unit 4 — putting it here
  would repeat the w2s2 mistake exactly.
- Ratio as a fraction of the whole beyond one gentle mention; it is a
  Unit 4 bridge.
