# Grace's Spanish — oral protocol (the spoken half)

> Status: READY TO RUN · 2026-10-18 · Runner: Timothy (or any adult)
> Run it within a week of the written checkpoint, either order.
> Takes ~10 minutes. Print or open on a phone; score as you go.

## Why this exists — read this first

**The written checkpoint cannot hear her.** The platform has no
audio, so the written score says nothing about: whether she
understands SPOKEN Spanish, whether she'll risk saying anything out
loud, or what her pronunciation instinct is. A written score alone is
roughly half the picture — anyone reading the family-folder results
should know the other half lives here. Listening and speaking are
assessed by a human or they are not assessed.

## How to run it

- Somewhere relaxed, not homework-shaped. Frame it as a game:
  "I'm going to be the lady in the Spanish café. Let's see what
  happens." Carmen from her checkpoint postcard can turn up too.
- Say each Spanish line naturally, not slowed to a crawl. Repeat once
  if asked — asking for repetition is itself a skill; note it, don't
  penalise it.
- STOP after two levels in a row where she gets nothing. End on a win:
  go back and redo a level she enjoyed.
- Score each item 0 / 1 / 2:
  **0** = no response or English guess unrelated to the Spanish ·
  **1** = partial (gets the gist, answers in English, or responds
  with a gesture) · **2** = appropriate response (English answer
  that proves understanding scores 2 on LISTENING items; Spanish
  words only required where marked SPEAK).

## The script

### Level 1 — The greeting (A0 listening)
| # | You say | A 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ¡Hola! (wave) | hola back, or hello + wave |
| 2 | ¡Adiós! (wave) | bye-wave, adiós |
| 3 | ¿Sí o no? — while offering something, e.g. a biscuit | a clear yes/no answer in any language |

### Level 2 — The café (A0–A1 listening)
You're the waiter. Props help (any cup, any plate).
| # | You say | A 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | ¿Chocolate o limonada? | picks one (either language) |
| 5 | ¿Agua? (offering a glass) | understands it's water |
| 6 | Un helado para Grace. (hand it over) | knows she just got an ice cream |

### Level 3 — SPEAK: her turn (A1 production)
| # | Prompt (in English) | A 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | "Order a water from me — in Spanish." | agua (por favor even better) |
| 8 | "Say thank you." | gracias |
| 9 | "Now order anything you like, in Spanish." | any Spanish food/drink word, however pronounced |

### Level 4 — The conversation (A1+ — expected ceiling)
| # | You say | A 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | ¿Cómo te llamas? | me llamo Grace / "Grace!" / "that's asking my name" |
| 11 | ¿Cuántos años tienes? | tengo diez (años) / "ten!" / shows ten fingers |
| 12 | Yo tengo cuarenta y ocho años. (point at yourself, adjust to your age) | laughs/reacts showing she caught a big number about you |

### Level 5 — Out of reach on purpose (A2)
Say it once, normal speed: **«Mañana vamos a la playa por la
mañana.»** Ask: "any idea what I just said?" Anything about
beach/tomorrow/morning scores; nothing is the expected result and
that's fine — say so out loud: "that one was meant to be too hard."

## Scoring sheet

| Level | Items | Score (…/2 each) | Notes (exact words she said) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Greeting | 1–3 | /6 | |
| 2 Café listening | 4–6 | /6 | |
| 3 Speaking | 7–9 | /6 | |
| 4 Conversation | 10–12 | /6 | |
| 5 Ceiling | 13 | /2 | |

Also note, in words (these matter more than the numbers):
- **Willingness**: did she TRY to speak, or freeze? (steers how much
  speaking Unit 1 asks of her)
- **Pronunciation instinct**: Spanish-ish vowels or full English?
  (information only — never a correction target at this stage)
- **Repetition requests**: asked for repeats = good strategy, note it.
- Her best moment, verbatim — it becomes an early-win callback in
  Unit 1.

## Where the results go

Type the filled sheet (scores + notes) into a file and commit it as
`docs/results/gracees-oral-YYYYMMDD.md`, or email it to the family
folder address — either way it must end up readable next to the
written results. The rubric (`gracees-rubric.md`) reads both halves
together; neither alone decides anything.
